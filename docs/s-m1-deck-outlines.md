# S-M1-01 to S-M1-04 — deck outlines

M1 is week 1, Monday 18 January 2027, and it is the only module whose decks
were never written. These are outlines to author against.

## What already existed, and what is new here

**What the corpus actually carries about these four decks is four titles.**
Doc 08 §6.3 names them — layers; compartments and ligaments; vessels and
danger zones; skin — and schedules them 17th to 19th of 21, deliberately last,
on the reasoning that the video library covers the ground adequately in the
interim. The Primer Gaps review then lists "anatomy and danger zones" under
*what is already covered well*, crediting `S-M1-01` to `S-M1-04`, the cadaver
library and `L-M1-01`. Three of those four did not exist when it was written.
M1 was counted rather than built.

So the slide-by-slide outlines below are **new work, not retrieved**. What they
are built from is sourced, and each line says where:

- M1's topic list, from the Learner Journey: five layers, fat compartments,
  ligaments, arteries and danger zones, facial nerve, muscle dynamics, skin and
  Fitzpatrick.
- The **eleven concepts** signed in the clinical review, which are the real
  teaching spine of M1 — concepts 01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 07 and 08 all land here.
- The clinical review's **commissioned entries** and its corrections, including
  the frontalis/corrugator inversion, the deep medial cheek fat sitting on the
  maxilla rather than the zygoma, and the tear-trough resolution.
- **Policy A4**: six millimetric figures may be published and no others. Three
  of the six are upper-face margins and belong in `S-M1-03`; the rest belong to
  later modules.
- The **nine danger zones**, six present in the dataset and three absent.
- The published deck pattern: 8–22 slides, stable `<deck code>/<slide id>` in
  the footer, continuous narration in `<aside>`, `[PAUSE]` where the player
  stops, figures pointing at the approved protocol rather than printed.

Every clinical line here needs the clinical lead's review before delivery, on the same
footing as the 112 rows already in the review register.

## Why these four, in this order

M1's delivery sequence is **slides → cadaver video → interactive → reference**,
and the pack's own line is that in M1 this is not a preference: it is the
difference between the library teaching and the library impressing. Each deck
therefore ends on a **watch-for** slide that carries into the cadaver cut it
precedes, which is the PL-13 feature already agreed.

The four decks are not four topics of equal weight. `S-M1-03` is the one the
gate rests on and the one a patient's eyesight rests on; it is the longest and
should be authored first.

## S-M1-01 · The five layers

*12 slides · precedes `V-B-TOPO`, the whole-face layer sequence · sign-off:
clinical lead*

The first deck a learner meets in the programme. Its job is to make "five
layers" a thing they have seen rather than a list they have read, and to plant
concept 01 before any needle appears.

| Slide ID | Slide |
|---|---|
| `cover` | Title, module, the one line: what is under the needle, in order |
| `ask` | Opening question: you are about to inject. Which plane are you in, and how do you know? |
| `stack` | The five layers, surface down: skin · superficial fat · muscle · deep fat · bone. Read as a stack, with what runs through each |
| `govern` | **Concept 01.** Plane and depth govern both the effect and the risk. Not two decisions — one |
| `stated` | And neither is verifiable at the bedside. A stated depth records intent and training, never position. Narration, palpation and tactile bone contact are all assertions |
| `regional` | The stack is not the same thickness everywhere. Temple against lip against lower eyelid, as a comparison |
| `nerve` | The facial nerve: where it runs superficial, and what that costs. The temporal branch and the marginal mandibular, taught as territory |
| `muscle` | Muscle dynamics. The face moves, so a plane is a moving target, and a resting assessment is half an assessment |
| `pause` | **[PAUSE]** A needle is at the lateral brow, 4 mm deep. Name the layers above and below it, and say which one you want |
| `errors` | The three mechanisms this module exists to prevent, named here and traced later: eyelid ptosis, brow drop, asymmetric smile. Each is a plane story |
| `watch` | **Watch for** in `V-B-TOPO`: where the superficial fat ends; how thin the temple envelope is; the first moment the dissection reaches bone |
| `legal` | Educational use, scope of practice, the clinical authority, © 2026 Prentice1 |

## S-M1-02 · Fat compartments and ligaments

*12 slides · precedes the regional cadaver cuts · sign-off: clinical lead*

Why the face ages in pieces, and why product does not go where the diagram
suggests it might. This is the deck where the clinical review's deferral of the
eight retaining-ligament entries has to be honoured: four lines survive, inside
this deck, and none of them becomes a subject of its own.

| Slide ID | Slide |
|---|---|
| `cover` | Title and the one line: the face is compartments, not a continuous mass |
| `ask` | Why does a face hollow in specific places rather than deflating evenly? |
| `superficial` | The superficial compartments, named, as a map |
| `deep` | The deep compartments, and the correction that matters: the deep medial cheek fat overlies the **maxilla**, not the zygomatic body. Over the zygoma the deep compartment is the SOOF. Medial projection is maxillary; lateral malar projection is on the zygoma |
| `fixed` | **Concept 07.** Fixed anatomical points constrain where product goes and where it must not. Ligaments do not let product cross them; foramina are exit points to be bounded, not approached |
| `fourlines` | The four ligament facts a beginner uses, and nothing more: filler above a ligament does not cross it · the tear trough and orbicularis retaining ligament complex is why the lid–cheek junction is a groove · the anterior masseteric border carries the facial artery and vein, a stay-away line · the facial artery crosses the mandibular border at the antegonial notch, so no deep bolus there |
| `orl` | Teach the tear trough and the ORL as one complex. The micro-CT dispute over whether they are one structure does not change what the needle does |
| `consequence` | What follows for planning: the nasolabial fold does not efface from above, and filler over a malar mound makes the mound worse |
| `vector` | Support before volume. Each placement has a vector, and the vector is the reasoning |
| `pause` | **[PAUSE]** A patient asks for the fold softened. You place product above it and nothing changes. Which structure explains that, and what do you do instead? |
| `watch` | **Watch for** in the regional cuts: the compartment septa as the dissection crosses them; the lid–cheek junction in the tear-trough cut |
| `legal` | Educational use, scope, clinical authority, © 2026 |

**Not in this deck, by decision:** lateral orbital thickening, malar septum,
zygomatic cutaneous ligament, temporal ligamentous adhesion, platysma-auricular
ligament. The clinical review deferred all five as having no beginner
application. They do not become slides, and nothing cites them.

## S-M1-03 · Vessels and danger zones

*15 slides · the deck the M1 gate rests on · author first · sign-off: clinical
lead, with the vision-loss pathway reviewed against `L-M1-01`*

The longest and most consequential M1 deck. Its argument, from concept 03, is
that a vessel map is the wrong primary model, because a map invites a learner
to read unmarked tissue as safe tissue.

| Slide ID | Slide |
|---|---|
| `cover` | Title and the one line: unmarked tissue is not safe tissue |
| `ask` | You can see where the arteries are drawn. What about the ones that are not? |
| `model` | **Concept 03.** Vascular risk runs by region, plane and angiosome, not by named vessel. The region-and-plane model is primary; the vessel overlay is an adjunct to it |
| `variance` | **Concept 08.** Surface landmarks are zones, not points. Where a standard deviation exceeds its mean, the region is published and the number is not. Palpate and confirm per patient |
| `six` | The six vessels the trainer can draw, with their territories: supratrochlear (glabella, forehead) · angular (nasolabial, medial cheek, nose) · facial (lower face, nasolabial, lips) · superficial temporal (temple, lateral brow) · superior labial (upper lip) · inferior labial (lower lip) |
| `three` | The three that are taught and **cannot be drawn**: supraorbital (forehead, brow) · dorsal nasal (nose) · infraorbital (tear trough). The overlay says so rather than omitting them, because an absent line reads as an absent artery |
| `deepnotsafe` | **Concept 02.** Deep is not categorically safe. Supraperiosteal placement is right in the malar region and a named danger zone over the infraorbital foramen. Depth is chosen per region, never as a policy |
| `trough` | The tear trough, resolved: deep supraperiosteal **medial to the infraorbital foramen**, small aliquots, cannula, low pressure. The danger zone is the foramen, not the plane |
| `anastomosis` | How the facial and ophthalmic systems connect, and why that connection is the whole problem |
| `visionloss` | The retrograde pathway, step by step, ending at the retina. This slide is what `I-M1-03` rehearses and `L-M1-01` teaches; the deck states it once, in order, so the learner meets it here first |
| `occlusion` | **Concept 04.** Occlusion is recognised as a time-ordered bundle, and no single sign rules it in or out: visual symptoms → disproportionate pain → blanching → livedo → delayed refill. Act on the first |
| `lidocaine` | **Concept 05.** Local anaesthetic blunts the cardinal sign. With lidocaine-containing filler, colour change rather than pain is the early signal — comfort and recognition pull against each other |
| `margins` | The three upper-face margins policy A4 permits, each against the complication it prevents: ≥1 cm above the bony supraorbital rim for corrugator, against eyelid ptosis · ≥1.5 cm lateral to the lateral canthus and ≥1 cm from the bony orbital rim, against lip ptosis via zygomaticus major · the 4–6 hour hyaluronidase window. No other figure appears in this deck |
| `pause` | **[PAUSE]** A patient reports sudden visual change during a glabellar treatment. What is your first action, and what is the second? |
| `watch` | **Watch for** in the cadaver cuts: how little tissue covers the supratrochlear at the brow; the variability of the labial arteries between sides |
| `legal` | Educational use, scope, clinical authority, © 2026 |

## S-M1-04 · Skin

*10 slides · precedes the skin cut and `V-A-ANATOMY` palpation episodes ·
sign-off: clinical lead*

The envelope every injection passes through, and the deck that stops
"assessment" meaning one kind of face. It sets up `S-M5-06` rather than
duplicating it.

| Slide ID | Slide |
|---|---|
| `cover` | Title and the one line: you inject through this, every time |
| `ask` | Two patients, the same plan. Why does one bruise, one blanch, and one show the product? |
| `envelope` | Epidermis, dermis, subcutaneous: the injection envelope, and what each layer does to a needle and to a result |
| `quality` | Skin quality by region — thickness, laxity, sebaceous character. Temple against lip against lower eyelid, where the differences change technique |
| `fitzpatrick` | The Fitzpatrick scale: what it was built to predict, what it does predict, and what it does not. It is a sun-response scale, not a map of ancestry |
| `diverse` | Sex, age and ancestry change the assessment. There is **no validated universal ratio** for any feature, and this deck publishes none — the corrected teaching behind DEF-07 |
| `pigment` | Post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation: who is at risk, what raises the risk, and what changes in technique and aftercare |
| `pause` | **[PAUSE]** Your patient has Fitzpatrick V skin and wants the under-eye treated. What changes about your plan, and what do you say about it? |
| `watch` | **Watch for** in the skin cut and the palpation episodes: dermal thickness between regions; how the envelope behaves under a cannula |
| `legal` | Educational use, scope, clinical authority, © 2026 |

## Build notes

**Author `S-M1-03` first.** The gate rests on it, two of its concepts appear
nowhere else in the programme, and it is the only deck here whose content
protects eyesight.

**Three slides carry a correction the review made and the platform has not.**
`S-M1-01/errors` and `S-M1-03/margins` carry the corrugator reasoning that
replaces the frontalis/corrugator inversion; `S-M1-02/deep` carries the
maxilla-not-zygoma correction; `S-M1-04/diverse` carries the lip-ratio
correction. The lesson stops that state the originals were descoped with their
errors intact, so until those stops are retired or re-authored the platform
teaches both versions. That is a dependency between this authoring job and the
stop backlog, not a separate problem.

**The three undrawable vessels are a content requirement, not a rendering bug.**
`S-M1-03/three` exists because the trainer's danger mode cannot draw the
supraorbital, dorsal nasal or infraorbital arteries. A learner who sees six
lines and is told nothing concludes there are six. The deck names all nine and
says which three the model cannot show.

**Figures.** Only the three A4 margins listed in `S-M1-03/margins` appear across
all four decks. Everything else points at the approved protocol, as the
published decks already do.
